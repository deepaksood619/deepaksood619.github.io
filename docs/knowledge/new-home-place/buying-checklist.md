### Area Terminology (Carpet / Built-up / Super Built-up)

- carpet area / floor space / usable floor space
- [Watch this before buying a FLAT | How to find fair market value of property - YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qCMFlfBTWks&ab_channel=LabourLawAdvisor)
- Carpet area / rera carpet area
- Built up area (10-15% more than carpet area)
- Super built up area - 20-50% more than built up area

#### Key Considerations

- **Focus on carpet area**: Prioritize the carpet area as it represents the actual usable space within the property.
- **Comparison based on carpet area**: When comparing properties, rely on carpet area measurements to get a clear understanding of the usable space.
- **Understanding built-up and super built-up areas**: Be cautious of these areas listed by builders, as they include additional non-usable spaces.
- **Ratio inquiry**: Ask for the ratio of carpet area to built-up and super built-up areas to better understand the space distribution.
- **Cost consideration**: Assess the cost per square foot of carpet area to make a more informed purchasing decision.
- [Difference between carpet area, built-up area, super built-up area](https://housing.com/news/real-estate-basics-part-1-carpet-area-built-up-area-super-built-up-area/)

### Building Lifespan, Redevelopment & UDS (Undivided Share of Land)

Every building has an "expiry date":

- **RCC (Reinforced Cement Concrete):** most modern high-rises are RCC. With proper maintenance (waterproofing etc.), lifespan is ~50-70 years; without maintenance, structural problems (cracks, seepage) can start in just 30-40 years.
- **Old load-bearing buildings** (pure brick/masonry): shorter lifespan, ~30-35 years.
- **Mivan technology:** not a magical, immortal construction technique — it's RCC construction using aluminum formwork instead of wood, casting walls and slabs together for a faster, stronger, smoother monolithic structure. Lifespan is still in the normal RCC range, and renovating/modifying walls inside a Mivan flat is much harder.

**What happens when a building "expires"** — four usual paths:

1. **Renovation/retrofitting** — if the core structure is still reasonably strong, structural repairs can extend life by another 10-20 years.
2. **Redevelopment** (most common/beneficial) — a builder demolishes the old building and constructs a new one; original owners usually get a new, often slightly larger, flat in the new building, sometimes plus cash compensation.
3. **Demolition** — if the building is completely unsafe and redevelopment isn't feasible, it's demolished and the land is freed up.
4. **Government acquisition** — government may acquire old land parcels for infrastructure development, paying compensation to owners.

**UDS (Undivided Share of Land) — the most important concept:**

- Buying an apartment also buys a registered share of the land the building stands on — the UDS.
- Over ~40 years, the physical building's value depreciates to almost zero, but the land's value appreciates multifold.
- During redevelopment, your compensation or share in the new project relies entirely on your UDS, not the size of your old flat.
- Low-rise, lower population-density buildings generally offer much higher UDS per flat than dense high-rises.

**Actionable checklist for flat owners/buyers:**

- Check your UDS from the sale deed, or ask the builder/society.
- If the building is older than 25-30 years, get a structural audit done to determine safety and remaining lifespan.
- Check the society's sinking fund — a weak fund leads to big struggles when major repairs or redevelopment talks begin.
- Before buying resale (especially a ~30-year-old flat), ask about its UDS and any redevelopment plans — an old flat with high UDS in a prime location can be an investment jackpot.
