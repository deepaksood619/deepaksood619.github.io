A framework for deciding whether a foreign degree is worth it, from a Raj Shamani interview with Dr. Karan Gupta (an HBS alumnus and admissions counselor). Figures are the speaker's own claims and some are uncertain (auto-captions).

## Decision Order

- Decide the career map first, then the course, then the specialization, then the university; the country comes last
- Rankings only produce a shortlist; judge each school by its published graduate outcomes (employers, startups), since universities are audited and outcome data is the single source of truth
- **"Five removals" test:** strip away the brochure, the brand and logo, the famous people, family and societal expectation, and the job fantasy; if the course and faculty still attract you, go
- **Do not go** with an average profile, no clarity and heavy debt for an unknown university, or just to leave India. **Do go** with a strong profile, a top university and mapped odds, even with a loan

## Profile Archetypes

| Profile | Suggested route |
| --- | --- |
| Safe-bet 70% student, about ₹25L budget | Indian MBA instead (ISB is about ₹24L CTC) |
| Top-10% researcher, about ₹1 Cr | US (STEM OPT for 3 years, then H-1B) |
| Nursing or health student, about ₹15L a year | Mix of US, UK and Europe |
| PR-chaser | Australia or New Zealand (Canada: easy PR, hard job; US: easy job, hard PR) |
| Prestige hunter | US Ivies, plus Imperial, NUS, IE |
| 30+ professional | MBA (peers have 7-12 years of experience) with a STEM or quant concentration |

- **Money-saving trick:** a 3-year UK bachelor's (about ₹15-20L a year, under ₹50L total) followed by a 1-year US master's instead of a 4-year US bill of about ₹3 Cr
- **Underrated destination:** Spain (post-graduation entrepreneurship visa; IE, ESADE)

## Course and Degree Value

- Pair any field with data, AI or tech (behavioral economics, econometrics, bioinformatics, legal data science, HCI, marketing analytics) and still learn Python to debug AI output. Renewable energy, cybersecurity and healthcare were called evergreen, while marketing and generic CS are no longer safe
- A degree is worth brand plus judgment (critical thinking, not information) plus network
- ROI is not just tuition against salary payback (Indian placement-cell thinking); add X (job, career or business) and Y (transformation, network and agency that compound over 5-10 years). Outside India there are career-management centers, not placements

## Policy, Visa and Funding

- Policy changes constantly (Canada's study caps, Australia's repeated rule changes), so never base the decision on current visa policy. Admission, visa, job and PR are four separate decisions by four different entities, so avoid anyone promising a guarantee
- **Funding:** bank loans up to about ₹1 Cr (capped at 80% of costs; co-signer-free loans only for top schools), TA/RA assistantships (tuition plus stipend), need-based aid at need-blind Ivies, Erasmus Mundus (about €1,400 a month), and Tata and JN Tata scholarships
- **Visa:** top-university admits face very low rejection (speaker opinion), obscure schools get grilled, and the US checks public social media for political activity while the UK and Europe do not
- **Counselor vs agent:** agents are paid by universities, so "free counseling" is a red flag; verify via the university site and alumni, and check MCI/NMC recognition for foreign medical degrees

## Profile Building and Job Search

- **Profile building:** three coherent boxes, namely capable (leadership, sports, activities), credible in the major (research, internships, courses) and a memorable passion project
- **Job search "MATCH":** Map the market (extract common skills from 20-30 job descriptions with AI), Assemble evidence (gap analysis, not "fix my resume"), Target the full ecosystem of firms, Combine networking with AI (write outreach yourself), Hone skills (tough AI mock interviews). Start networking on day one
- **Returning to India:** global capability centers (GCCs) were cited at about 2,100 centers with about 2.36M employees and above-average pay, a good return path; work 4-7 years abroad if desired, or return for India's growth

## Frameworks

- **SSF (Security, Status, Freedom)** explains parent-student conflict; use [5 Whys](psychology/soft-skills/asking-right-questions.md) to find the real motive behind wanting to study abroad
- About 58% of international students report loneliness (UK survey cited), so plan for it
