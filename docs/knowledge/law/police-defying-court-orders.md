If police in India fail to release someone despite a court order (including the Supreme Court), it is a serious legal violation with multiple remedies. When the whole state machinery colludes, the problem becomes a question of separation of powers: the judiciary has neither the purse nor the sword.

## Key Points

- Contempt of Court (Art. 129 SC, Art. 215 HC): up to 6 months imprisonment, fine, or both
- Habeas corpus (Art. 226 HC, Art. 32 SC): detention becomes illegal, court orders the body produced
- Departmental inquiry via DGP / Home Secretary; suspension, dismissal, pension forfeiture
- Criminal prosecution under BNS: wrongful confinement, public servant disobeying law
- Compensation under Art. 21, often recoverable from the officers' salaries
- If the inquiry is a sham: CBI/SIT transfer, Police Complaints Authority, NHRC/SHRC, private complaint to Magistrate (Sec. 223 BNSS, earlier Sec. 200 CrPC)
- If compensation is not paid: contempt petition against Home/Finance Secretary, attachment of state property

## Escalation When Police Protect Their Own

- **Transfer to an independent agency:** CBI or an SIT of retired judges / outside officers
- **Contempt by senior officials:** DGP / Home Secretary can be held in contempt for a biased inquiry; Chief Secretaries and DGPs have been summoned in person
- **Police Complaints Authority (PCA):** required in each state after the *Prakash Singh* judgment
- **NHRC/SHRC:** own investigative wing, can recommend prosecution and compensation
- **Private criminal complaint:** Magistrate takes cognizance and issues summons directly against police officers
- **Forcing compensation:** personal appearance of Home/Finance Secretary; threat of contempt conviction; attachment of bank accounts, vehicles, state property in extreme cases

## Total Breakdown: Legislature + Executive + Police Ignore Courts

Known as a Constitutional Crisis. The judiciary has neither the power of the purse nor the sword (Hamilton: "neither force nor will, but merely judgment").

- **State level:** Art. 355 and 356, President's Rule, central forces deployed to enforce court orders
- **Central level:** President's oath (Art. 60) to preserve, protect and defend the Constitution; Supreme Commander of armed forces (military intervention borders on a coup)
- **Loss of legitimacy:** civil disobedience, bureaucratic fracture (IAS/IPS oath is to the Constitution)
- **Electoral backlash:** e.g. 1975-77 Emergency, voters ousted the government at first opportunity
- **International isolation:** investment flight, sanctions, asset freezes

## How Other Democracies Handle It

- **Federalism (USA):** states control their own law enforcement and can sue the federal executive
- **Judicial case management:** discovery orders, briefing schedules, amicus curiae, court-appointed defenders, post-trial injunctions to force executive transparency
- **Legislative accountability:** no-confidence vote, power of the purse, Question Hour, Zero Hour, parliamentary committees
- **International law:** treaties with domestic force, suits in foreign courts, refusal of extradition/recognition, UN sanctions, asset freezes

## Thought Experiments: Moving Purse or Police to the Judiciary

- **Judiciary controls the purse:** no democracy does this. Democratic deficit (unelected, unaccountable judges), no macroeconomic expertise, corruption just shifts from politicians to judges
- **Judiciary controls police and appointments:** loss of neutrality (judging its own employees), same opacity as the Collegium system, "who judges the judges?". Madison: accumulation of all powers in the same hands is "the very definition of tyranny"
- **Legislature holds purse, judiciary runs the executive:** end of impartial justice, administrative paralysis (rule by injunction), hyper-politicised judicial appointments, legislative micromanagement of budgets
- **Government awards contracts, judicial executive clears payments (pre-audit):** bottleneck, contractors inflate bids, conflict of interest when a scam is revealed later, shadow off-budget funding

## CAG (Comptroller and Auditor General): The Post-Audit Solution

Supreme audit institution under Article 148, the guardian of the public purse. Democracies prefer independent post-audit over a judicial lock on the treasury: government spends, CAG audits and publishes reports (2G, Coalgate), courts prosecute later.

- **Appointment:** by the President on advice of the PM and Council of Ministers
- **Term:** 6 years or age 65, whichever is earlier
- **Removal:** only like a Supreme Court judge, two-thirds majority in both Houses for proved misbehaviour or incapacity
- **Financial independence:** salary and expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund of India (no annual vote)
- **Post-retirement ban:** barred from any further office under Union or State governments

## Why Not Just Add More Judges?

Trials themselves act as punishment when they run for years.

- **Vacancies:** SC sanctioned strength raised to 38 (2026), working strength 37; High Courts sanction 1,120+ with about 25% vacant; lower courts have thousands of vacancies
- **Infrastructure deficit:** no courtrooms, stenographers, clerks, IT or housing for new judges
- **Archaic procedure:** endless adjournments, appeals against minor interim orders, no cross-examination timelines
- **Who controls strength and budget:**
    - Supreme Court: strength by Parliament (Supreme Court Number of Judges Act), funded by the Centre
    - High Courts: strength by the President on Centre's advice; pensions from Centre, salaries and administration from States
    - Subordinate courts: almost entirely States, in consultation with High Courts
- **Root problem:** most states allocate under 1% of their budget to the judiciary; courtrooms do not win elections

## Fixes Used Elsewhere

- **Budget independence:** Netherlands Council for the Judiciary (formula budget tied to caseload); Costa Rica constitutional fixed percentage (about 6%); USA judiciary submits budget directly to Congress, unaltered by the President
- **Pre-trial management:** US discovery leads to over 95% of civil cases settling before trial
- **Loser pays (UK/Europe):** deters frivolous and harassing suits
- **Professional court managers:** judges only judge, MBAs/operations staff run calendar, HR, IT (India's attempt has stalled)

## India's Implementation and Results (as of mid/late 2026, per source)

Overall pendency is still growing: SC pending crossed about 95,000 (Aug 2026); NJDG shows over 3.2 crore pending cases across the judiciary.

- **Adjournment caps:** CPC max 3 per party; BNSS caps criminal adjournments at 2
- **BNSS timelines:** judgment within 30 days (extendable to 45) of arguments, charges framed within 60 days of first hearing, investigation within 90 days for offences under 7 years; in practice "aspirational" due to about 30% vacancies and a lack of forensic labs and prosecutors
- **Mediation Act 2023:** mandatory pre-litigation mediation for civil and commercial disputes
- **e-Courts Phase III:** 4.15 crore video-conference hearings, 1.2 crore e-filed cases, 38 lakh charge sheets transferred via the Interoperable Criminal Justice System, eSummons via email/WhatsApp
- **AI and virtual courts:** SUPACE for judges' research; petty traffic cases handled in virtual courts
- **Bottom line:** a modern digital highway with legislated speed limits, but the traffic jam will not clear until states fund the system and hire enough judges
