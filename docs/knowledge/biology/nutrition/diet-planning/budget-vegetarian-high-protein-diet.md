73% of diets in India are protein deficient. A high-protein vegetarian diet does not require whey or paneer-level spending — 110g of protein/day is achievable for ~₹150 using soya chunks, thick lentils, chickpea flour (besan), milk and curd.

## Protein Requirement (ICMR)

- ICMR: healthy adult needs 0.83 g protein per kg body weight (minimum to avoid deficiency)
  - 60 kg → ~50 g/day
  - 70 kg → ~58 g/day
- Active individuals / gym-goers need more than the ICMR minimum

## Protein Quality Matters, Not Just Grams

- Egg and milk — gold standard, almost completely absorbed
- Soya — champion of the plant world, complete protein (all 9 essential amino acids)
- Wheat/rice protein — low quality and incomplete
- Lentils + rice is technically a complete amino acid profile, but the typical Indian plate (thin dal, more water, rice-heavy) is ~90% carbs and very little protein — the fix is **thick dal, less rice**, not the pairing itself

## Cheapest High-Quality Protein Sources

| Source | Protein (per 100g) | Price | Cost per g protein |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soya chunks | 52 g | ₹100-140/kg | ₹0.20-0.30 |
| Thick lentils (dal) | - | - | ₹0.40-0.70 |
| Chickpea flour (besan) | 20 g | ₹80-100/kg | - |
| Milk (250 ml glass) | 8 g protein | - | - |

- Lentil protein alone is incomplete (low in methionine) — combine with curd + a little rice to complete the amino acid profile
- Besan chilla + paneer topping increases protein and completes the amino profile

## Sample Day: 110g Protein for ~₹150

**Breakfast — ₹20, 28g protein**

- Soya bhurji (50g soya chunks → 26g protein) + 1 glass buttermilk (2-3g protein)

**Lunch — ₹30, 18g protein**

- Thick lentils (50g dry dal, less water → 12g protein) + a little rice (3g protein) + 100g curd (3.5g protein)

**Evening snack — ₹40, 24g protein**

- Besan chilla (50g besan → 11g protein) with 70g paneer topping (13g protein) — complete amino profile

**Dinner — ₹30, 32g protein**

- Soya curry (50g soya chunks → 26g protein) + 1 millet roti — jowar/bajra/nachni (3g protein) + 100g curd (3.5g protein)

**Before bed — ₹15, 8g protein**

- 1 glass milk (250 ml) — slow-digesting casein, helps overnight recovery

Total: ~110g protein for ~₹150 (a ₹25 buffer is kept since prices vary by location/brand).

## Common Mistakes

- **Dry vs cooked weight confusion:** protein numbers (e.g. 50g soya = 26g protein) are for dry weight. Soya roughly triples to ~150g after soaking/cooking — don't recount cooked weight as if it were dry.
- **Front-loading protein into one meal:** distribute protein across every meal instead of eating little all day and a large amount at dinner — muscle protein synthesis responds better to per-meal signals.

## Myths Addressed

1. **Soy drops testosterone / causes gyno** — Multiple human meta-analyses show normal soy intake (50-100g chunks/day) does not lower testosterone, raise estrogen, or cause gynecomastia.
2. **Vegetarians can't get complete protein** — 110g of vegetarian protein for ₹150/day demonstrates otherwise.
3. **Whey/supplements are necessary** — Whey is convenient and high quality, but not necessary. Real food first (soy, thick lentils, milk); powder only if training is serious and food alone isn't hitting the target.
4. **High protein damages kidneys** — No scientific evidence of kidney damage from high protein intake in people with healthy kidneys. Those with pre-existing kidney disease should follow medical advice.
5. **Paneer is the best veg protein** — Paneer protein quality is comparatively better, but it's expensive; soya is the more budget-efficient hero for hitting a high protein target cheaply.
