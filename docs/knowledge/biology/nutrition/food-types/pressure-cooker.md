Pressure cookers are not used as universally in Western countries as they are in India because of different cooking styles, kitchen appliances, and historical safety fears.

## Why It's Not Popular Outside India

- **Boiling vs. roasting** — Indian cooking relies heavily on boiling, stewing, and making gravies (dal, curries, rice), which fit a pressure cooker perfectly
- **Western techniques** — Western and many other global cuisines focus more on baking, roasting, frying, and grilling, where a pressure cooker isn't useful
- **Ovens and slow cookers** — Western kitchens are often built around ovens, stovetop pans, and slow cookers, used instead of pressure cookers to roast meats or simmer stews over long hours
- **Fear of explosions** — older generations in Western countries remember stories or accidents involving old stovetop pressure cookers blowing their lids off
- **Loss of control** — many home cooks prefer pots where they can easily see and stir the food while it cooks, rather than sealing it under high pressure

Note: traditional stovetop "whistling" pressure cookers are rare in places like the US or UK, but multi-cookers like the Instant Pot have recently made pressure cooking popular again there.

## Traditional Stovetop vs. Electric Multi-Cooker

- **The "whistle" count** — Indian stovetop cookers rely on a weight valve that vents pressure by lifting and whistling; Indian recipes are usually written by counting whistles (e.g. "cook for 3 whistles"). Electric multi-cookers are silent and cook based strictly on digital timers
- **Heat control** — stovetop cookers require manually adjusting the flame from high to low; electric cookers adjust their own internal temperature automatically using built-in sensors
- **Safety features** — traditional cookers have a simple safety valve that melts or pops if pressure gets too high; electric multi-cookers feature over a dozen digital safety mechanisms, including lid-locking pins and auto-shutoff sensors, making them nearly impossible to explode

| Feature | Traditional Indian Stovetop Cooker | Modern Electric Multi-Cooker |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanics | Manual flame adjustment | Automated digital controls |
| Monitoring | Requires counting audible whistles | Requires setting a digital timer |
| Materials | Primarily aluminium or stainless steel | Heavy stainless steel or non-stick inner pots |
| Versatility | Only pressure cooks and sautés | Pressure cooks, slow cooks, makes yogurt, steams, and bakes |
| Portability | Requires a stove burner | Plug-and-play anywhere with a power outlet |
| Speed | Heats up and builds pressure faster | Takes longer to heat up and build pressure |

## Why Stovetop Cookers Still Dominate in India

- **Speed and power** — Indian stovetop cookers heat up much faster on high gas flames than electric elements
- **Habit** — Indian home cooks are highly accustomed to cooking by the sound of whistles
- **Cost** — traditional aluminium or steel cookers are significantly cheaper and last for decades without needing electronic repairs
