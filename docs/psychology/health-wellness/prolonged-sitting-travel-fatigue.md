Struggling to sit through long car rides or flights is an incredibly common issue, often called "travel fatigue." Because you're confined to a small space in a moving vehicle, your body deals with physical, circulatory, and sensory stressors all at once.

## Ergonomic & Muscular Strain

- **The "C-Curve" problem:** most car and airplane seats sink in the middle, forcing your body into a "C" shape. This flattens the natural inward curve of your lower back, putting pressure on lumbar discs and muscles.
- **Sciatica & nerve compression:** sitting for hours without a break compresses the sciatic nerve and lower spine discs, causing a dull ache, sharp shooting pain, or an inability to sit still.
- **Muscle shortening:** with hips and knees bent at 90 degrees for hours, hip flexors and hamstrings stay in a shortened position and stiffen/ache.

## Restricted Circulation

- **Blood pooling:** gravity and bent knees restrict blood flow from the legs back to the heart, so fluid pools in the lower legs, making them feel heavy, swollen, and uncomfortable.
- **Micro-adjustments & vibration:** cars and airplanes vibrate constantly at low frequencies; core and spinal muscles make thousands of invisible micro-adjustments per minute to keep posture stable, leading to deep muscle fatigue over several hours.

## Sensory & Neurological Triggers

- **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS):** a neurological condition causing a deeply uncomfortable, uncontrollable urge to move the legs while resting. Confined spaces like airplanes and cars are major triggers.
- **Sensory conflict (motion sickness):** if discomfort includes nausea, dizziness, or exhaustion, the inner ear senses motion while the eyes (looking at a stationary seat back or screen) signal stillness — this sensory clash is physically exhausting.

## Environmental Factors (Flights)

- **Cabin pressure & dehydration:** airplane cabins have low humidity and lower-than-normal oxygen levels, dehydrating muscle tissue and reducing oxygen to the brain, which amplifies aches, stiffness, and fatigue.

## Quick Fix

Place a rolled-up sweater or small travel pillow behind the small of the lower back. This pushes the spine out of the stressful "C" shape and can immediately relieve lower-back pressure.
