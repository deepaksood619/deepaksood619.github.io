Emodiversity is a psychology concept meaning the experience of a rich variety of emotions - positive and negative - rather than only trying to maximize positive ones. It reframes "well-being" away from the popular idea that the goal of life is to be happy.

## Key Points

- The idea that "the goal of life is to be happy" is misleading; well-being is not about maximizing happiness but about feeling the full range of emotions.
- Sadness, anger, and fear are not "villains" - every emotion is necessary and serves a purpose.
- People who experience higher emodiversity have been found to have better mental and physical health, according to research.
- Pixar's *Inside Out* popularized this idea narratively - the film's point was that suppressing "negative" emotions in favor of constant happiness is harmful.
- Modern life (doomscrolling, social media) causes rapid "emotional context switching" - within minutes a person can move through fear, envy, aspiration, insecurity, and outrage, often without realizing it.
- Nearly 65% of Gen Z reported struggling emotionally or mentally in the past 2 years, which raises the relevance of this concept for younger generations.
- A simple practice: pause and check in with your emotional state before reacting (e.g., before continuing to scroll, snapping at someone, or assuming the whole day is ruined based on a fleeting feeling).
- Awareness of what you're feeling - even unpleasant emotions - creates a sense of choice and control, which is a more achievable definition of well-being than "trying to be happy all the time."
