## DICGC deposit insurance (applies to banks only)

- Cover is ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, principal and interest combined, pooled across all accounts the same person holds at the same bank (savings, FD, RD). Separate per bank and per depositor.
- NBFC FDs (Bajaj Finance, Mahindra Finance, Shriram Finance) are not covered. The credit rating is the only protection.
- A rise to ₹7.5 lakh was reported but is unconfirmed. Treat ₹5 lakh as the limit.
- A ₹5L FD at about 8.6% for 3 years grows past ₹5L, so full cover needs principal of about ₹3.9L per person per bank. Existing balances at the same bank count against the same limit (check the Shivalik FD already in the debt sheet).

## ICICI Pru Corporate Bond (Direct Growth) vs Bajaj Finance FD 8.15%

Inputs: fund 5Y CAGR 6.69% (trailing, after expense ratio). FD 8.15% senior-citizen rate, treated as an effective annual yield. Tax at a 20% slab plus 4% cess = 20.8%. Fund units bought after Apr 2023 are taxed at slab on the gain at redemption (Sec 50AA), so tax is deferred to exit. FD interest is taxed every year as it accrues, even on the cumulative option. Per ₹5L, tax on the FD paid from the accruing interest (equivalent to reinvesting after tax).

| Horizon | FD post-tax CAGR | Fund post-tax CAGR | FD value on ₹5L | Fund value on ₹5L |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 yr | 6.45% | 5.30% | ₹5.32L | ₹5.26L |
| 3 yr | 6.45% | 5.37% | ₹6.03L | ₹5.85L |
| 5 yr | 6.45% | 5.43% | ₹6.84L | ₹6.51L |
| 7 yr | 6.45% | 5.50% | ₹7.75L | ₹7.27L |
| 10 yr | 6.45% | 5.58% | ₹9.35L | ₹8.61L |

Pre-tax the gap is 1.46 points (8.15% vs 6.69%). After tax it narrows only to about 1.0 point at 5 years. Deferral helps the fund by only about 0.03 points of post-tax CAGR per extra year held, not enough to close the gap at this return level.

Break-even: to match the FD's 6.45% post-tax, the fund would need a pre-tax CAGR of about 8.0% over 3 years, 7.9% over 5 years, or 7.7% over 10 years. The trailing 6.69% falls short at every horizon.

Sensitivity to the tax rate (5-year horizon, post-tax CAGR):

| Slab (with cess) | FD | Fund at 6.69% |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | 8.15% | 6.69% |
| 5.2% | 7.73% | 6.38% |
| 10.4% | 7.30% | 6.07% |
| 20.8% | 6.45% | 5.43% |

The FD stays ahead at every slab, because deferral only matters at a high tax rate and a long hold. The parents' actual slab may be far lower than 20%, so rerun with their real rate. TDS thresholds for senior citizens also apply (verify the current limit).

Caveats that change the picture:

- The 6.69% is trailing, not a forecast. ICICI Pru Corporate Bond's portfolio YTM was 7.83% (30 Sep 2026), TER about 0.32%. If rates stay flat, a forward return near 7.5% is plausible, which is roughly 6.1% post-tax at 5 years (6.4% on the full 7.83%). That is still under the FD's 6.45%. A fall in yields would add price gains, a rise would cut them. Modified duration is 3.39 years.
- The FD rate is locked for the tenure, and early exit carries a penalty. The fund can be exited any day in any amount with no lock-in but with NAV risk.
- The FD is unsecured NBFC credit (AAA rated, not insured). The fund holds AAA-heavy corporate paper but with market-price risk and no fixed maturity.
- The household's own XIRRs, as of 1 Oct 2026: ICICI Pru Corporate Bond 6.78% (₹5L from 2020-11-29), HDFC Corporate Bond 6.36% (₹8L from 2021-10-08).

Net: on the numbers, the 8.15% FD wins post-tax at every horizon tested. The fund's remaining edge is liquidity and flexible exit, not return.

## ICICI Pru Corporate Bond Fund: holdings, risks, can it beat 8.15%

- Rating profile about 72% AAA, 23% sovereign, 0.6% AA (MTNL, government-guaranteed credit enhancement).
- Top five corporate issuers (NABARD, SIDBI, LIC HF, REC, PFC) are about 36%, all government-linked financiers.
- Holds Bajaj Finance paper itself (0.67%).
- Yield to maturity (YTM) 7.83%, direct expense ratio 0.32%, modified duration 3.39 yrs, Macaulay 3.56, average maturity 5.92 yrs.
- Returns, Regular Growth: 1Y 5.30%, 3Y 7.07%, 5Y 6.44%. Direct 5Y CAGR 6.69%.

### Can it beat the 8.15% FD?

- Expected net carry is about 7.5% (7.83% YTM less 0.32% expense ratio). Beyond a holding period longer than the ~3.5 yr duration, return converges to roughly the starting yield, so about 7.5% pre-tax is the central case for 5 years or more.
- One-year return is roughly net carry minus (change in yield x 3.39). Parallel-shift estimate, ignoring convexity and roll-down:

| Change in yields | Approx. 1Y return |
|---|---|
| -0.50% | ~9.2% |
| -0.25% | ~8.4% |
| -0.19% | ~8.15% (break-even) |
| 0 | ~7.5% |
| +0.25% | ~6.7% |
| +0.50% | ~5.8% |
| +1.00% | ~4.1% |

- After tax at 20.8%: FD nets 6.45%. Fund at 7.5% nets about 6.1% over 5 years even with deferral. Break-even is about 7.9% pre-tax over 5 years.

### Underperformance risks, by importance

1. Rising rates. One source reports an RBI repo hike of 0.25 points to 5.50% in early Oct 2026 and a 10Y G-sec yield around 7.23% (two-year high), with Goldman expecting more hikes. The fund's 1Y return was 5.30% at 30 Sep, an aggregator showed 4.76% on 9 Oct.
2. The 17% in long government bonds drives most of the volatility.
3. Structural drag: expected net yield sits below 8.15% before anything goes wrong.
4. Credit and structure risk is small: 8.2% securitised pools and the MTNL guarantee-backed bonds.
5. Redeeming during a rate spike locks in a loss.

Fund's advantages: exit any day, any amount, no exit load, no early-withdrawal penalty. It can beat the FD in a year when yields fall.

Data gaps: no clean Nov 2026 NAV or 1Y return found, aggregator figures conflict across plan types, no current corporate-bond spread view.
