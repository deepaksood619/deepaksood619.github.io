The same ₹50 lakh of business profit can attract ~₹8L, ~₹11L, ~₹12.5L or 24L+ of tax depending on the entity it is earned through - proprietorship, partnership, Private Limited or HUF. This note walks through how each entity is taxed and which one minimises tax. Source: Labour Law Advisor (Rishabh Jain) video - figures below are per the video, under the new tax regime.

**Applicable:** FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) onwards · Income Tax Act 2025 (Section 58 replaces 44AD/44ADA)

## Key Points

- **Business** = anything related to commerce, trading or manufacturing. **Profession** = vocation - earning from skill, education or craft. Some professions are **specified professions** (doctors, lawyers, interior designers, IT consultants, etc.) - this matters for presumptive taxation.
- Business income is taxed on **profit**, not revenue: `Revenue (receipts) − eligible expenses − depreciation = Profit Before Tax (PBT)`; PBT − tax = PAT.
- **Eligible expenses** are those related to the business (stock purchased, shop rent, electricity/water, staff, warehousing). A Vegas casino trip is not.
- **Depreciation** - capital assets (mini truck, furniture, AC) are expensed over their useful life, e.g. a ₹15L truck over 5 years → 20% per year.
- Two ways to compute profit: **actual books** or **presumptive** (declare a fixed % of receipts as profit, no books/vouchers needed).
- A **proprietorship has no separate identity** - it uses the proprietor's PAN, and all businesses (restaurant, shop, YouTube, clinic) are clubbed into one individual's income. Partnership, LLP, OPC, Pvt Ltd and HUF each have a **separate PAN** and are separate taxable entities.
- Presumptive taxation is available to **Individual, HUF and Partnership firm only** - **not to LLP or Pvt Ltd/OPC** (they must maintain books and get audited).
- Old sections **44AD / 44ADA** are now **Section 58** under the Income Tax Act, 2025.

## Entity Comparison

| Entity | Separate PAN | Tax rate on profit | Presumptive (Sec 58 / 44AD / 44ADA) | Profit withdrawal | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Proprietorship | No (owner's PAN) | Individual slab rates + surcharge (`>₹50L`) + 4% cess; 87A rebate up to ₹60,000 (income ≤ ₹12L) | Yes | No extra tax - it is already the owner's income | Small businesses / professionals |
| Partnership Firm | Yes | Flat 30% + 12% surcharge if profit `>₹1 Cr` + 4% cess (max ~34.94%) | Yes | Partner salary/remuneration taxed at partner's slab; **share of profit after tax is exempt** | Profitable businesses that distribute profits |
| LLP | Yes | Same as partnership - 30% flat | **No** - books + audit required | Same as partnership | Partnership with limited liability |
| Private Limited / OPC | Yes | 22% (Sec 115BAA) + surcharge + cess = **25.168% effective** | **No** - books + audit required | Dividend taxed at shareholder's slab (surcharge capped at 15%); salary taxed at slab | Funded / loss-making startups, or long-term reinvest-and-exit |
| HUF | Yes | Individual slab rates | Yes | - | Ancestral property rent, family business |

## Presumptive Taxation (Section 58, earlier 44AD / 44ADA)

| | Business (44AD) | Specified Profession (44ADA) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Turnover / receipts limit | ₹2 Cr (₹3 Cr if cash receipts ≤ 5%) | ₹50L (₹75L if cash receipts ≤ 5%) |
| Minimum profit to declare | 6% of digital receipts + 8% of cash receipts | 50% of gross receipts |
| Books of accounts | Not required | Not required |

These are **minimums** - you can declare more.

**Examples:**

- ₹2 Cr revenue (₹1 Cr online + ₹1 Cr cash) → min profit ₹6L + ₹8L = **₹14L**
- ₹3 Cr revenue (₹2.9 Cr online + ₹10L cash, cash `<5%`) → min profit **₹18.2L**
- A doctor / designer / IT consultant with ₹50L receipts cannot declare 6% - must declare at least 50%.

## Proprietorship - Tax on Profit

Taxed exactly like an individual (new or old regime slabs). Up to ₹12L income gets 87A rebate up to ₹60,000; surcharge applies above ₹50L (10% for ₹50L-₹1 Cr); 4% cess on the total.

| Profit | Tax |
| --- | --- |
| ₹10L | Slab tax ₹40,000 - fully rebated under 87A → **₹0** |
| ₹50L | No rebate, no surcharge → **~₹11.23L** |
| ₹1 Cr | Slab tax + 10% surcharge + 4% cess → **~₹29.5L** |

## Partnership Firm

Flat **30%** on taxable profit. Partners can be paid salary/remuneration/commission (deductible for the firm), but within limits on **book profit**:

| Book profit slab | Max partner remuneration |
| --- | --- |
| First ₹6L (or loss) | Higher of ₹3L or 90% of book profit |
| Balance above ₹6L | 60% |

- Book profit ₹3L → 90% = ₹2.7L, but ₹3L is higher → all ₹3L can be paid → taxable profit **₹0**
- Book profit ₹10L → ₹5.4L (90% of 6L) + ₹2.4L (60% of 4L) = **₹7.8L** remuneration; **₹2.2L** taxed at 30%
- **Most beautiful benefit:** remuneration is taxed in partners' hands at slab rates, but the firm's **profit after tax distributed to partners is exempt** in their hands.

**₹1 Cr book profit, 2 partners at 50:50:**

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Max remuneration | ₹61.8L (₹30.9L each) |
| Firm taxable profit | ₹38.2L → tax ~₹12L |
| Firm PAT distributed | ~₹26L (₹13L each) - exempt |
| Tax on each partner's ₹30.9L salary | ~₹5.27L |
| **Total tax on ₹1 Cr** | **~₹22.5L** |

If firm profit exceeds ₹1 Cr: 30% + 12% surcharge (= 33.6%) + 4% cess → ~34.94% max.

## LLP

Tax maths and 30% rate are **exactly the same as a partnership firm**. The only catch: **no presumptive taxation** - LLP and Pvt Ltd have heavy compliance anyway, so the government requires actual books, actual profit and audit (if in range).

## Private Limited / OPC

Base rate 22% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess = **25.168% effective** - ₹10L profit → ~₹2.5L tax. Looks cheaper than 30%, but the money is still inside the company.

**Extreme example - OPC with ₹100 Cr profit:**

| Route | Company tax | Tax in director's hands | Total tax | Director keeps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keep in company, pay out as dividend | ~₹25.17 Cr | ~₹26.8 Cr (30% slab + surcharge capped at 15% for dividends + cess) | **~₹52 Cr** | ~₹48 Cr |
| Take all as salary (needs board/MCA approval) | ₹0 | ~₹39 Cr (₹75K std deduction, 30% slab, 25% surcharge, 4% cess) | **~₹39 Cr** | ~₹61 Cr |
| Same profit in a partnership firm | 30% + 12% + 4% ≈ 34.94% | PAT exempt | **< ₹39 Cr** | more |

**So why do people choose Pvt Ltd?**

- Loss-making startups that need funding / equity investment - no choice.
- Long-term vision: founder doesn't withdraw (has other income), **reinvests** the profit (only ~25% tax each year leaves more to reinvest), and eventually exits by selling shares / listing - paying only **capital gains tax** (video says ~20% on unlisted shares; post Budget 2024 LTCG on unlisted shares is 12.5%; lower still for listed).
- But if you run a **profitable business and withdraw profits**, partnership or even proprietorship is far more tax-efficient than Pvt Ltd.

## HUF

- Limited business types: managing ancestral property (rental income) or a family business / shop / cottage industry.
- Taxed like an individual (same slabs), **but no 87A rebate** - ₹10L income → ₹40,000 tax for HUF vs ₹0 for an individual.
