## Question

If foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) pulled all their money out of India, where would the Nifty 50 go, and how much could domestic investors absorb?

Short answer: domestic money can absorb a steady drip (₹1-2 lakh crore a month) with a 10-20% correction, but not a wholesale exit at current valuations. The gap has to clear through lower prices.

## Supply vs Absorption

- FPI holdings: ~15.1% × ~₹450 lakh crore ≈ **₹68 lakh crore** (assumed market cap).
- DII buying: ₹6.83 trillion in about 9.5 months, so ~₹8-9 trillion a year annualised.
- At constant prices, a full exit would take ~**8 years** of current DII buying to absorb. Within one year, domestic money can absorb ~12-15% of FPI holdings.
- The rest must clear through price: valuations fall until household money moves from gold, real estate and FDs into equities, and until insurers, EPFO and NPS raise equity allocation.

## Calibration From 2026

DIIs bought ~₹6.8 trillion against ₹2.4-3 trillion of FPI selling (more than double), yet the Nifty is still ~12% below its late-2024 high (~26,277). Domestic flows absorbed the volume but not at constant prices.

## Scenarios (from Nifty 23,146)

| Scenario | Fall | Nifty level |
|---|---|---|
| Partial exit (~25% of FPI holdings, ~₹17 lakh crore) | 10-20% | ~18,500-20,800 |
| Full exit over ~1 year | 25-40% | ~14,000-17,400 |
| Full exit in 1-3 months (disorderly) | 40-55% | ~10,400-13,900 |

These are judgement-based ranges, not forecasts. Reference episodes:

- **2008:** Nifty about -52%, with only ~₹52k crore of FPI net selling but a global crisis.
- **March 2020:** about -38% in one month, with ~₹1.1 lakh crore of FPI selling.
- **Oct 2024 - Feb 2025:** about -16% on ~₹2-3 lakh crore of FPI selling, while DIIs bought more than FPIs sold.

## Why It Could Be Worse Than Simple Supply/Demand

1. **Concentration:** FPIs hold ~35% of the Nifty 50 free float and ~45-50% in names like HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank, so large-cap banks would take the biggest hit.
2. **Pro-cyclical domestic flows:** SIPs have held in past drawdowns but are not guaranteed. A 40% crash could cut retail flows and trigger mutual fund redemptions.
3. **Rupee:** FPIs convert proceeds to dollars, so the rupee would fall sharply. The RBI would draw on its reserves, which tightens liquidity.
4. **Feedback loops:** margin calls, pledged-share liquidations and F&O unwinds.

## Why a Full Exit Is Unrealistic

FPIs are not one actor. Sovereign funds and long-only passive trackers are sticky, and index-mandate holders (EM or global benchmarks) exit gradually. A full exit would need a trigger such as capital controls or a tax shock, which would also hit domestic sentiment.
