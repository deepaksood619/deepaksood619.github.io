Debbie Welsh, 57, sold her Los Angeles house, car, and belongings for $550,000 and relocated to Bali with her 82-year-old mother (82). She now lives on about $1,300/month (plus `~$380`/month from a YouTube channel and romance writing), versus needing `~$2,000`/month just to get by in LA on a $120,000/year consulting income. This is a real budget breakdown from her "Cost of Living" episode — a useful geographic-arbitrage data point for retirement/FIRE planning.

## Key Points / Monthly Budget Breakdown (USD)

- **Housing:** 1-bed/1.5-bath, `~1,100` sq ft, 10-year lease paid upfront (`~$8,000`/year averaged, including $35,000 in renovations over the lease); mother's house on a separate 4-year lease, `~$350`/month ($16,800 paid upfront).
- **Utilities & maintenance:** `~$75`/month utilities (water, gas, electric, phone, internet) + `~$60`/month property maintenance (pool, AC, water delivery, waste) + `~$45`/month maid (once a week).
- **Transport:** Owns a scooter ($500 one-time) costing `~$6`/month in gas/maintenance; supplements with Go-Jek/Grab taxis at `~$22`/month (her) and `~$33`/month (mother) — a point-to-point ride costs `~$7`.
- **Food:** `~$310`/month groceries (local/organic items much cheaper than imported Western goods); eats out 2-3x/week at `~$7`/meal.
- **Healthcare:** International health insurance `~$124`/month with a $10,000 deductible (emergencies only); local clinic visit `~$15`, medication $2-6; mother's US Medicare ($200/month) does not work in Bali. A full-time in-home caregiver, if ever needed, would cost `~$500`/month total.
- **Visa:** Her 2-year visa cost $732 total; her mother's 1-year visa costs `~$1,000`/year (visa costs change annually).
- **Pets:** Vet house-call `~$10`, vaccinations `~$50`; dog food `~$3`/kilo (lasts 3 days); cat food is comparatively expensive.

## Key Lessons

- Lifestyle creep is the biggest risk: "you can unafford yourself living here as well" — costs feel so low at first that people keep adding small expenses until the savings advantage erodes.
- Local/organic goods are cheap; imported Western goods carry most of the inflation — shifting consumption toward local goods is what makes the low budget work.
- Leases are often paid entirely upfront (a year, 4 years, even 20 years) — a very different cash-flow model than monthly Western rent, and worth planning for before relocating.
- Losing a career identity was a bigger adjustment than the cost-of-living change — she described her job as her "purpose" and had to rebuild identity and social ties after the move.
